Quick guide

What's your VDOT?

VDOT is a single number for your current running fitness, worked out from a race result. Coach Jack Daniels and physiologist Jimmy Gilbert created it from lab tests on runners, and it's the system behind Daniels' Running Formula. The higher the number, the fitter you are.

It isn't your lab VO₂max. It's your "effective" VO₂max: what your race time says about your engine and your running economy together. Two runners with the same VDOT can expect similar race times.

How to use it

  • Enter a recent all-out race (last 4–6 weeks), ideally between 1500 m and the marathon.
  • Use an official chip time if you have one. A training run or a hilly, very hot race will underestimate you.
  • Re-check every few weeks. As your race times drop, your VDOT goes up and your paces get faster.

What you get

EEasy
Most of your weekly running: easy days, long runs, recovery. Conversational.
MMarathon
Your predicted marathon race pace, for steady blocks in long runs.
TThreshold
"Comfortably hard": tempo runs and cruise intervals. Raises your lactate threshold.
IInterval
Hard 3–5 minute reps that work your VO₂max. Equal jog recovery.
RRepetition
Short, fast 200–400 m reps for speed and form, with full recovery.

Equivalent race times show what the same fitness should give you at other distances. They're handy for setting goals, and they assume you've trained for that distance.

Good to know

  • Predictions assume a flat course and cool weather. Heat, humidity and hills slow everyone down, so expect to be a little slower on a hot Punjai morning.
  • Most of your week should be Easy. Daniels' rough limits for the hard work: Threshold about 10% of your weekly distance, Interval about 8%, Repetition about 5%.
  • New to running? Build a few weeks of easy running first, and check with a doctor if you have a health condition.

Formula: Daniels & Gilbert, Oxygen Power (1979). VO₂ = −4.60 + 0.182258·v + 0.000104·v² (v in m/min); share of VO₂max held for t minutes = 0.8 + 0.1894393·e−0.012778t + 0.2989558·e−0.1932605t; VDOT = VO₂ ÷ share. Results match the published Daniels tables to within a few seconds.
